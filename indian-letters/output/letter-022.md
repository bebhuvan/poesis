*Mridu, Mridu’s uncle and Babof have

been here for the last three days. Babo has become

quite friendly with me this time. He is the picture of

health. He joins me in playing on the Japanese monk’s

drum. This monk is a gem, frank, humble, cheerful and

courteous. He is learning Hindi and spins oil the charkha

and the takli. He scrupulously observes all the Ashram

rules. I have given many hours to the girls [Mani and

Mridula]. Both are in great hurry, and as they

travel by plane, have threatened to leave by the mail

train today. Mani’s leg seems to require electrical treat¬

ment. Mridula wants to go and nurse some friends of

hers. Both seem to get along fairly well with each other.

There is a cable from Subhas today that Vitthal¬

bhai’s body will reach Bombay on the ninth November

and that the cremation rites be performed by yourself.

My reply through the press has been that I do not

expect you to ask for release on parole, and so the cre¬

mation would have to be done in your absence^
