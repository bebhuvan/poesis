Letter LXII

Wardha
August 22, 1934

Here is a problem to be solved by you, Kaka wishes

to go and work in the South. I suggested to the teachers

who have come here that they should live in villages

and do constructive work including basic education.

They liked the idea and those who can be relieved are

prepared to carry it out. Kaka is one of them.,..

Mathuradas will definitely go and meet Andrews on

Saturday, oend others also if they can make it conve¬

nient to go. If possible, please arrange to have him stay

with you and let him leave for here the same day if

he so wishes.

LXII I

W ardha

August 23, 1934

I enclose a letter about the dissensions in Delhi;

tear it up when you have read it. I have flatly refused

to have anything to do with it. Such news keeps coming

in every day. Everyone for himself and the devil" take

the nation. Such being the prevailing mental attitude,

I cannot see how we can reach our goal. I have also

received complaints against Aney from Bengal. I have

clearly written back that his impartiality is above

suspicion, and they should repose full faith in him.

Malaviyaji has issued orders to the Hindustan Times to

modify its policy about the Communal Award and there¬

fore Ghansliyamdas [Birla] has sent his resignation with

arguments in lavour of his own opinion. Wait and see

wnat happens now. One fails to understand how their

minds are working.

O

CONGRESS NATIONALISTS

You will see in the papers a statement issued to the

Associated Press by me in connection with Rajendra-

babu’s telegram. It would be a good idea if you also

keep issuing similar statements from time to time.

Maulana [ Abul Kalam Azad ] telegraphed asking how

they can call themselves the Congress Nationalist Party.

I wired back to say that this question could only be

answered by the Congress President; that I might write

something about it but he should approach you. Now

see if you hear from him.

I enclose Rajaji’s letter as it is worth reading. You

may write to him if you feel like it. Go to Madras

if you have the time and the energy. I have not seen

the Statesman. It may write anything whatever, but how

can we conceal the truth? Praphulla Ghosh is here;

his account of the corruption in Bengal is distressing.

From Letters to Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel by M.K. Gandhi

Published by Navajivan Publishing House, Ahmedabad (1957)
