A FREE INDIA AND A FREE CHINA. Dear Generalissimo, I can never forget the five hours close contact I had with you and your noble wife in Calcutta. I had always felt drawn towards you in our fight for freedom, and that contact and our conversation brought China and her problems still nearer to me. Long ago, between 1905 and 1913 when I was in South Africa, I was in constant touch with the small Chinese colony in Johanssesburg I knew them first as clients and then as comrades in the Indian passive resistance struggle in South Africa. I came in touch with them in Mauritius also. I learnt then to admire i their thrift, industry, resourcefulness and internal unity. Later in India I had a very fine Chinese friend living with me for a few years and we all learnt to like him. I have thus felt greatly attracted towards youlr great country and, in common with my countrymen, our sympathy has gone out to you in your terribfe struggle. Our mutual friend, Jawajj^lal Nehru, whose love of China is only excelled, irat all, by his love of his own country, has kept us in intimate touch with the developments of the Chinese struggle.
