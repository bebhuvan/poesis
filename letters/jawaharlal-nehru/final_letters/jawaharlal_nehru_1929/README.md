# Letters from a Father to his Daughter (1929)

**by Jawaharlal Nehru**

---

## About This Collection

This historic collection contains 31 letters written by Jawaharlal Nehru to his 10-year-old daughter, Indira Priyadarshini Nehru (later Indira Gandhi, Prime Minister of India), during the summer of 1928.

At the time, Indira was staying in Mussoorie in the Himalayas, while Nehru was in Allahabad in the plains below. These letters were his way of continuing their conversations about the world, history, civilization, and humanity's journey through time.

### Historical Significance

- **Written:** Summer 1928
- **Published:** 1929 by Allahabad Law Journal Press, Allahabad
- **Author:** Jawaharlal Nehru (1889-1964), later First Prime Minister of India (1947-1964)
- **Recipient:** Indira Gandhi (1917-1984), later Prime Minister of India (1966-1977, 1980-1984)
- **Indira's Age:** 10 years old when these letters were written

These letters formed the foundation of what would later become Nehru's famous work "Glimpses of World History." They represent a father's attempt to educate his daughter about the broad sweep of human civilization, from the formation of Earth to ancient India.

### From Nehru's Foreword (1929)

> "These letters were written to my daughter Indira in the summer of 1928 when she was in the Himalayas at Mussoorie and I was in the plains below. They were personal letters addressed to a little girl, ten years of age. But friends, whose advice I value, have seen some virtue in them, and have suggested that I might place them before a wider audience.
>
> I hope that such of them as read these letters may gradually begin to think of this world of ours as a large family of nations. And I hope also, though with diffidence, that they may find in the reading of them a fraction of the pleasure that I had in the writing of them."

### Educational Approach

Nehru wrote these letters with remarkable care for a young child's understanding:
- Uses simple analogies (pebbles smoothed by rivers, mahouts commanding elephants)
- Builds from concrete to abstract concepts
- Encourages curiosity and questioning
- Promotes humanistic values and seeing humanity as one family
- Emphasizes learning from nature and evidence

### Legacy

These letters are significant not just as historical documents, but as:
- Early science education for children in India
- Humanistic approach to world history
- Example of engaged parenting during political struggle (Nehru was increasingly involved in the independence movement)
- Foundation for Indira Gandhi's worldview and later political philosophy
